Taxes

Keep the most of every payout: 2026 and 2027 rules, in plain words

In one minute

NOT ADVICE
  • MFFU payouts are pay for a service, so they are self-employment income. Nobody takes tax out for you.
  • You pay income tax plus 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of your profit (about 14% of profit).
  • Eval fees, resets, data, platform and a business share of the PC and internet lower the profit.
  • Pay four times a year. Next date: Jan 15, 2027.
  • The rule: move 30% of every payout to a separate tax pot the day it lands (Maryland: 30% for our expected year, 40% if you also have a day job: table below).
  • Then: 10% eval reserve, 10% retirement, 20% live-account fund, the rest is yours.

How your payouts are taxed

The form
A 1099 (normally 1099-NEC) in late January or February. Paid through Rise: Rise mails it. Paid by bank (Plaid/ACH): MFFU mails it.
No form?
From 2026 a firm only has to send one at $2,000 or more. The money is taxable either way.
Where it goes
Schedule C (profit or loss) and Schedule SE (self-employment tax) with your Form 1040.
SE tax
15.3% = 12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare, on 92.35% of profit. Half of it is deducted from your income.
Not 60/40
The futures 60/40 rule is for your own contracts. A payout is the firm paying you, so treat it as normal income.
QBI
Likely up to 20% of the profit off your income tax (Form 8995); from 2026 at least $400 if you have $1,000+ of active business income. It does not lower SE tax.
Losing year
If fees beat payouts and you run it like a business, the loss can lower other income. Ask a pro.

What you can deduct

  • Eval fees, resets, activation fees (Builder 50K $91.80, 25K $63 / $43). Keep each receipt email.
  • Market data, NinjaTrader, VPS and other trading tools.
  • The PC: the business share. Up to $2,500 an item can usually be written off at once.
  • Internet and phone: the business share only (say 40%). Write down how you picked it.
  • Home office: only a space used regularly and only for the business. Simple way: $5 a square foot, up to 300 sq ft ($1,500).
  • Agency costs if you start it: domain, hosting, software, ads.

Not deductible: personal costs and the private share of anything.

Never pay a penalty

  • Your 2026 tax was $0? (a full year, no tax owed) Then there is no underpayment penalty for 2027 at all. You still owe the 2027 tax by Apr 15, 2028, so keep the pot anyway.
  • Otherwise: by each due date pay a quarter of 100% of your 2026 total tax (110% if your 2026 income was over $150,000). Then no penalty, however big 2027 gets.
  • Day job trick: raise the extra withholding on your W-4 at work. Withholding counts as paid evenly all year, even if you add it in December.
  • Pay from the pot on the four dates above, free at IRS Direct Pay. Keep every confirmation number.

Keep more: do this at $X a year

ladder
  1. Day 1, any amountSeparate savings for the tax pot. Save every MFFU receipt (eval fees, resets, data, NinjaTrader). Write down the business % of the PC and internet. Each $100 of fees saves you about $15 - $30 of tax.
  2. Under $15,000Your income tax is near $0, so a Roth IRA beats a deduction: up to $7,500 a year (2026 and 2027) goes in after tax and comes out tax free later. Open one free at any big broker.
  3. $15,000 - $30,000Open a free Solo 401(k) (Fidelity, Schwab, E*TRADE). Pick Roth while your bracket is 10-12%; pick traditional once part of your income is taxed at 22%. You can put in up to about 93% of the profit at this level.
  4. $30,000 - $60,000Solo 401(k), traditional on the part above the 12% bracket. Example: $6,000 in at $60,000 saves about $1,060 of federal + Maryland tax (about $1,670 with a day job). If you buy your own health insurance, it is deductible too. An HSA only if your health plan is a high-deductible one ($4,400 a year single).
  5. About $60,000+, steady for a yearTalk to a CPA about an S-corp. Break-even in Maryland is around $49,000 profit: below it the payroll, the extra return and the $300 report cost more than it saves. About $1,450 saved at $60,000, $2,600 at $100,000, $4,150 at $150,000. Check first that MFFU will pay a business.
  6. LLC?An LLC only protects your personal things; it changes no tax by itself (a one-owner LLC is taxed just like you now). In Maryland it costs $100 + $300 every year, so only worth it with the S-corp.

Retirement money is locked until about 59 1/2 (Roth IRA contributions, not growth, can come out any time). Deadlines: a sole owner can open a Solo 401(k) for a year even after Dec 31, up to the tax filing date (Apr 15, no extension), and put that first year's employee money in by then; employer money can wait until the filing date with extensions. A SEP-IRA can be opened up to the filing date with extensions but allows less at these incomes. 2027 limits: 401(k) $25,500, IRA $7,500, HSA $4,500.

When to open a live account

YOUR MONEY
The trigger
$5,000 in the live fund, all from payouts, and at least 4 payouts received. At 20% of each payout that is about 6 - 9 months.
Start at
1 MNQ per bot (LIT and ATLAS, at most 2 micros open). Add 1 micro per bot for each extra $5,000.
Stop rule
If the account falls to $2,500, stop live and go back to prop only.
Why $5,000
On the bots' real trades at 1 MNQ: chance of losing half in a year 0% in a 2026-like market, 9% if the market turns like 2021-25 (no edge). At $3,000: 1% / 25%. At $1,000: 14% / 60%.
What it makes
About $1,540 a month at 1 MNQ each in a Jul-Oct 2026-like market, about $120 in a 2021-25-like one. Backtests overstate.
Why it pays
Your own futures gains are taxed 60/40 (Form 6781) and have no self-employment tax. In Maryland, $30,000 of prop payouts with no other job: about $6,800 tax; $30,000 of live futures gains: about $1,840 (all Maryland, $0 federal). With a day job: about $9,700 vs $6,000.
Margin
Day-trading margin for 1 MNQ is $100 at NinjaTrader and AMP (NQ $1,000); holding overnight needs about $4,600 for MNQ, $46,000 for NQ (checked Nov 20, 2026). The bots close the same day. The pattern-day-trader rule was for stock margin accounts only (and is being retired); futures never had it.
Losses
A net 60/40 loss can be carried back 3 years against 60/40 gains (Form 6781). Prop fees can't.

Prop first, live as an add-on: a prop account risks only its fee ($43 - $97). Keep every MFFU slot full (max 2 funded Builder 25K, 1 Builder 50K, 5 funded in all); after the 5th payout a Builder moves to MFFU live and the sim accounts go dormant. Never trade against yourself across accounts (MFFU bans hedging). The live account stays paper-off until you arm it yourself.

Your state: Maryland

TELL CLAUDE
State tax
2% rising to 4.75% from $3,000 (5% from $100,000; new 6.25% / 6.5% top brackets above $500,000 / $1,000,000). Standard deduction $3,350, personal exemption $3,200.
County tax
On top: 2.25% to 3.30% depending on your county (most are 3.20%; Anne Arundel and Frederick use bands). It adds about $700 - $1,000 at $30,000.
SE income
Taxed at the same state + county rates. No extra Maryland self-employment tax.
Futures 60/40
Maryland has no lower rate for capital gains: your own futures gains are taxed like normal income (about 6% - 8% with the county), plus a 2% surcharge only if your income is over $350,000. Still far less than prop income.
Quarterly
Maryland wants its own estimates (Form 502D with a PV voucher, or online) on the same 4 dates as the IRS once you expect to owe over $500. No penalty if you pay 110% of last year's Maryland tax or 90% of this year's; late money costs about 0.9% a month. Pay the Maryland part from the same pot.
Retirement
Maryland starts from your federal income, so a traditional Solo 401(k), SEP or HSA lowers Maryland tax too.
LLC
$100 to form, then a $300 annual report every Apr 15 (even with no income). No tax change by itself.
S-corp
Maryland follows the federal S election (still the $300 report), plus Maryland unemployment tax (1% - 2.6% of the first $8,500 of your salary).

Tell Claude your county and whether you have a day job: the % above then becomes one exact number.

Records to keep

  • Every payout: date, account, amount, the Rise or bank statement.
  • Every fee, reset, data and platform receipt.
  • PC receipt, internet bills and the business % you used; home office size.
  • Your 1099s and each estimated payment's confirmation number.
  • Last year's return (for the no-penalty rule) and the Kalshi yearly file.

Keep them at least 3 years after you file. The Ledger app already holds your fees and payouts.

Get help free

  • VITA: free IRS volunteers, usually for income around $70,000 or less. Some do not take business returns with many costs, so ask when you book. Find one with the VITA locator on IRS.gov.
  • IRS Free File: free guided software if your income qualifies.
  • Above that, one visit a year to a tax preparer or enrolled agent is usually worth it for a business return with quarterly payments.
